Corporate gifting in India operates at the intersection of professional obligation, personal warmth, and cultural expectation. Get it right and the relationship deepens. Get it wrong and you spend time trying to recover from an impression you didn't intend to make.

This guide covers the cultural conventions, the practical etiquette, and the GST implications every gifting team in India should understand.

## The cultural context

In Indian business culture, the gift is not primarily a transaction. It is a gesture of relationship. It communicates that the giver sees the recipient as a person, not just a counterpart. This is why timing, presentation, and care taken with the gift matter as much as the gift itself.

Festive occasions are the most culturally significant gifting moments: Diwali, Eid, Christmas, and to a lesser degree Holi and Dussehra. Sending nothing on Diwali to a key client who sent you something is noticed. Sending something generic when the relationship warrants more is also noticed.

Outside festivals, gifts are appropriate for: client milestones (a company anniversary, a successful funding round, a significant deal close), professional milestones (a promotion, a retirement), and as thank-yous after a substantial engagement.

## What to give

**For senior clients:** Premium and personal. Something that reflects their known taste, their aesthetic, or their world. A fine whisky or wine. A handcrafted piece from an Indian artisan. A premium leather portfolio. A curated hamper with items sourced thoughtfully, not assembled generically.

**For mid-level relationships:** Curated but elegant. A quality hamper. A premium journal and pen set. A food selection from producers worth knowing about.

**For large client lists:** Invest in the packaging and the note even if the item is modest. A beautiful presentation of a considered item at Rs. 2,000 is better than a Rs. 5,000 generic hamper in a plain box.

## What not to give

**Anything with a prominent logo.** The gift should not look like a marketing exercise. Your brand can be present, but subtly. A blind-embossed logo on the outer box is elegant. A logo printed in full colour across the item is not.

**Cash or cash equivalents.** Gift cards from large retailers, digital vouchers, or anything that looks like cash-adjacent is received poorly in a gifting context. It feels transactional. If you want to give an experience, give a specific experience: a dinner reservation, a spa booking, a performance.

**Gifts that could create compliance concerns.** In regulated industries such as finance, pharma, and public sector, high-value gifts can be perceived as inducements. Know your company's gifting policy and, where relevant, the recipient's. A gift should never put either party in an awkward position.

**Gifts involving religion you are not certain about.** India is diverse. A gift with religious imagery that does not match the recipient's tradition can land poorly even when intended generously. Err toward secular.

**Perishables with unclear dietary alignment.** India has strong vegetarian, vegan, and halal traditions across different communities. If you are not certain of the recipient's preferences, stick to vegetarian and avoid alcohol unless you know it is welcome.

## On presentation

In Indian gifting culture, the presentation is part of the gift. A beautiful box, well-wrapped, with a handwritten or personally typed note inside, creates an impression before the recipient has even seen the contents. The unwrapping is part of the experience.

Do not shortchange this. If the gift is generous but the packaging looks like it was assembled in a rush, the packaging is what people remember.

## The GST dimension

For B2B gifting, the GST treatment matters.

Under Indian GST law, gifts to employees are taxable as a supply if they exceed Rs. 50,000 in value per employee per financial year. For client gifts, the treatment depends on whether the gift is "in the course of or furtherance of business."

Input tax credit on goods purchased specifically as gifts is generally not available. However, the recipient does not pay GST on receiving a business gift.

For large-scale gifting programmes, the cleanest approach is to work with a gifting partner who provides a full GST-compliant invoice with HSN codes, our GSTIN, and an itemised breakdown. This allows your finance team to correctly classify the expenditure and avoids complications during audits.

If you are in a regulated industry, consult your tax team on the specific treatment applicable to your circumstances. Rules in this area have evolved and continue to be clarified.

## On timing

Advance planning matters more than most buyers expect. For Diwali, the best sourced hampers and custom packaging run out. For personalised gifts, lead times are real. The gift that arrives two weeks after the festival because it was ordered at the last minute sends a message of its own.

## A final note

The best guide to client gifting etiquette in India is also the simplest: give something you would be proud to receive. If the gift you are sending is something you would look at and think "this is clearly from a company that didn't think about it," send something else.

The gift reflects the giver. Make sure yours reflects well.
